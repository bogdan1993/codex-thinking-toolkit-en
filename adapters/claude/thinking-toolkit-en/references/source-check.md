# source-check


## Inputs
Sources, links or sourced claims; optionally purpose and time period. Natural-language parameters are supported.

## Workflow
Inspect authorship, provenance, method, relevant expertise, date and connection to the claim. Prefer available primary sources. Identify documented financial or institutional interests and dependencies between sources: repeated copies of one press release are not independent confirmation. Flag weak secondary evidence and unsupported claims. Popularity does not establish validity. Distinguish documented interests from possible conflicts.

## Output
Assessment of each source and its effect on the conclusion. Follow the requested format and language; use English when no language preference is available.

## Missing information and composition
Ask only for missing information that materially changes the result; continue independent work and label assumptions. Do not claim actions, access, checks or results that did not occur. Combine relevant skills in a logical order, reuse evidence and avoid loops. Research externally only when needed or requested. Activating a skill does not itself authorize sending, publishing, installing or modifying external resources; follow the scope authorized by the user.

## Example
`/SOURCE-CHECK Assess whether these three articles provide independent evidence.`

## Acceptance
Meet the workflow and requested output, preserve constraints, and distinguish facts, interpretations, estimates and recommendations where relevant. Verify the result, not merely the existence of a file.
